A leader of the New Democratic Party (NDP) has urged the Canadian government to intervene against a controversial data-sharing agreement between U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and Thomson Reuters, a prominent media company in Canada. Avi Lewis, the NDP leader, expressed his concerns in a statement following reports from Canada's National Observer regarding the $125 million deal, which allows ICE to access Thomson Reuters' Clear database. This database includes sensitive information like property records, social media profiles, and geolocation data, purportedly to detect "voter fraud" and "immigration fraud".

Lewis articulated his alarm over the arrangement, stating, "My blood ran cold when I read about this development," reflecting the apprehensions many have about the partnership. He described it as an unacceptable level of complicity between Canadian corporations and ICE, calling for Ottawa to take action to prevent such collaborations. In his view, the federal government should block the contract and restrict Canadian businesses from engaging with ICE.

The deal marks a continuation of Thomson Reuters' relationship with ICE, which has been in place since 2015 when the company first provided access to the Clear database for tracking individuals for deportation. Human rights advocates have long criticized such agreements, warning that they contribute to the potential for abuse and violations of human rights.

The current contract is particularly contentious as it explicitly states that the Clear software will aid the Trump administration in addressing alleged voter fraud. During a recent televised address, the president made numerous unfounded claims regarding the integrity of the 2020 U.S. presidential election. Election officials from various political backgrounds have condemned these false assertions and reaffirmed the reliability of the election process.

Thomson Reuters, which also owns the news agency Reuters and the Globe and Mail newspaper, has not publicly commented on the specifics of its contract with ICE. However, a spokesperson referred to the company's description of Clear, claiming that it is only used for legitimate legal investigations and emphasizing that its tools are not intended for surveillance purposes.

In response to this agreement, Lewis proposed that the Canadian government should deny export permits for companies that sell equipment to ICE and cut public funding to businesses engaged with the agency. He previously called for action in January after the deaths of two U.S. citizens due to ICE operations in Minneapolis.

The office of Prime Minister Mark Carney has not yet responded to inquiries regarding the Thomson Reuters contract. Legal experts have noted that Canada could indeed impose restrictions on corporations engaging with ICE based on existing legislation. Joel Bakan, a law professor at the University of British Columbia, pointed to the Special Economic Measures Act, which permits the government to limit trade related to human rights abuses.

While it may be politically complex for the Canadian government to take such a stance against the United States, Bakan indicated that there is a legal foundation to support restrictions against entities like ICE, which have been accused of human rights violations.
