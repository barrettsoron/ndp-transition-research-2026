The Carney government has introduced legislation that would overhaul Canada's privacy laws — described as the first major update in the digital age. Per the accessible portion of the report, the bill is meant to curb surveillance pricing (without a sweeping ban on personalized pricing), makes it easier for Canadians to have companies delete or move their personal data, and adds transparency measures around algorithmic decision-making plus special treatment for children's information. Artificial Intelligence and Digital Innovation Minister Evan Solomon introduced the bill in the House of Commons on June 15, 2026.

This Toronto Star report covers the same legislation (Bill C-36) as the iPolitics article archived for this date, which carries the NDP's response. Surveillance pricing has been a signature NDP policy plank under Avi Lewis since the leadership campaign.
