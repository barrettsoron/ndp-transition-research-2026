Rolled coils of steel sit in the yard at the ArcelorMittal Dofasco steel plant in Hamilton, Ont., on Thursday. Nick Iwanyshyn/The Canadian Press

The prospective trade deal being negotiated by Ottawa and Washington would introduce a quota system for Canadian steel exports, with a 25-per-cent tariff on shipments inside the quota and a 50-per-cent tariff on exports above it, according to three sources with knowledge of the negotiations.

According to a steel industry executive, the proposed deal includes a tariff-rate-quota system for steel that would allow four million tonnes a year of the metal to be shipped to the U.S. at the lower rate.

Mark Rendell, Adrian Morrow, Marieke Walsh and Bill Curry report that after four million tonnes, steel exports will still be subject to a 50-per-cent tariff – the current duty Canadian steel exporters face under the Trump administration’s Section 232 sectoral tariffs.

Two other industry sources confirmed that the proposal on the table is a 25-per-cent tariff inside the quota. The steel executive added that the proposed deal would see Canada remove all of its counter-tariffs on U.S. steel and further restrict imports of the metal from third countries.

The Globe and Mail is not naming the sources as they were not authorized to publicly discuss the negotiations.

Canada is also negotiating the terms of aluminum trade, with the aim of reducing tariffs.

Negotiations are continuing, so things could change. Dominic LeBlanc, the minister responsible for Canada-U.S. trade, and chief negotiator Janice Charette met with U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer in Washington today, hours before the latest deadline to lock down details of a deal.

LeBlanc, Charette and Marc-André Blanchard, Prime Minister Mark Carney’s chief of staff, arrived at Greer’s office near the White House shortly after noon. The group did not respond to questions about the state of negotiations or when the contents of a deal would be made public.

Earlier this week, JD Vance said Prime Minister Mark Carney tried to “out-tough” U.S. President Donald Trump. The U.S. Vice-President credited Conservative MP Jamil Jivani, a long-time friend, as a helpful advocate for Canada.

In audio from a fundraiser held in Southampton, N.Y., which was provided to the Canadian Press by a source who attended the private function, Vance also said Carney is a “very sweet guy” who he gets along with quite well.

NDP MP Gord Johns "https://www.ourcommons.ca/members/en/gord-johns(89263)"), the member for the Vancouver Island riding of Courtenay-Alberni, shares his fiction and non-fiction picks for this summer, making him the latest MP to participate in the newsletter’s recreational-reading survey. Johns is a former businessman and municipal councillor from the Vancouver Island district of Tofino. He was first elected an MP in 2015.

***Finished this summer:***

**Atomic Habits: An Easy & Proven Way to Build Good Habits and Break Bad Ones by James Clear (Avery Publishing Group, 2018)** *Important to me because I serve a rural riding that is three time zones away from Ottawa, about 9.5 hours each way (door to door), is seven hours wide and serves 34 communities. The travel alone, on top of the 70 to 90 hours of work and travel a week, takes a huge toll on my mental, physical and spiritual health. It’s hard to get in a routine. This book, since the beginning of the summer, has already helped me develop some good habits that will improve my overall health and well-being.*

**Letting Go: The Pathway** **of Surrender by David R. Hawkins (Hay House LLC, 2014)** Johns said he reread this book this summer. *Really it’s about conscious behaviour and clarity. The House of Commons and politics often reflect unconscious behavior, and it’s important to see it so you don’t get trapped in it. I’ll reread this every year of my life. I think everyone (but especially politicians), should read this to keep them grounded in compassion.*

***Two that I’m almost finished:***

**Health for All: A Doctor’s Prescription for a Healthier Canada by Jane Philpott (Signal, 2024)** *As health critic, I’m trying to finish Jane Philpot’s book on improving our health care system and creating a universal primary care system across Canada. She’s* *got some great ideas**, I just wish our political leaders would embrace it.*

**Home Truths: Fixing Canada’s Housing Crisis by Carolyn Whitzman (On Point Press, 2024)** *It’s a Canadian author writing about the housing crisis. She explains how we got here, compares us to other countries that have had significant success in building to non-market housing, and highlights eras of success in our own history where we built non-market housing. It’s compelling in how building (or not building) non-market housing impacts our nation’s social and economic prosperity.*

***A book I was given by a constituent that I will read by the end of the summer:***

**Five Times Faster: Rethinking the Science, Economics and Diplomacy of Climate Change by Simon Sharpe, (Cambridge University Press, 2023)** *I have three children, which is motivating enough, but as my riding and the country has burned over the last three years, I’m hoping this book will help give me inspiration and ideas to help push to accelerate climate action in Parliament. Clearly, we need to do things differently and much faster.*
