Toronto Star coverage (Mark Ramzy, Parliament Hill Bureau) reporting that, as the Carney government signals a crackdown on **surveillance pricing**, major retailers are lobbying for weaker guardrails they argue are necessary for data-based personalized discounts. Critics counter that this is "a talking point used to justify a 'creepy' business practice that violates people's privacy and discriminates against the consumers who ultimately pay a higher price." An Instacart spokesperson is quoted saying the company wants to "educate the federal government on the most effective way to ban surveillance pricing without putting at risk many common, everyday affordability measures consumers care about."

Archived here because a ban on surveillance pricing is a signature plank of Avi Lewis's NDP platform; the article tracks the policy moving into Carney-government action and the resulting corporate pushback.
