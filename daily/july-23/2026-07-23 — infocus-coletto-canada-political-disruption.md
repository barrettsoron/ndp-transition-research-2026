Abacus Data CEO David Coletto analyzes a new [Abacus survey](https://abacusdata.ca/canadians-want-economic-and-political-change-but-most-are-not-looking-for-a-revolution/) on Canadian attitudes toward socialism, capitalism, democracy, and the appetite for upheaval, and argues it points to a latent opening for Avi Lewis's NDP.

## Key findings cited

- Four in ten Canadians think the economic system needs major or complete change; one in five wants a different system altogether. Among Canadians under 45, almost half say major or complete change is needed.
- A sharp generational divide on democracy: 78% of those 60 and over say democracy is always preferable, versus 43% of those aged 18 to 29.
- Younger Canadians report more openness to socialism, democratic socialism, communism, populism, and authoritarianism — which Coletto reads less as coherent ideology than as "the openness to alternatives," with the old stigma around such labels weaker for people without lived memory of them.
- Conservative and NDP voters are "equally likely to say the economic system needs major or complete change," though they want opposite remedies — public intervention and redistribution on the left, lower taxes and fewer constraints on the right.

## Argument on Avi Lewis

Coletto frames the survey's mood as an opportunity for Lewis, whose party "has struggled to grow beyond its existing coalition." In his words:

> This is where the opportunity for Avi Lewis becomes real.

He contends the results give Lewis "room to be more ambitious" — to argue that the affordability crisis is structural rather than temporary and that "the rules themselves need to change" — while Pierre Poilievre has a parallel opening from the same dissatisfaction on the right. Coletto attributes the current stability partly to Trump-era external threat pushing voters toward Mark Carney, cautioning that the political centre may not hold once that pressure eases: the stability, he writes, "should not be mistaken for deep satisfaction."
